What I built
Sameer SarnadBuilding AI Products

The bare minimum baseline.

  • Cleaned up the HTML and extracted the raw text, keeping parva, sub-parva, section and paragraph details
  • Fixed 1,000-character chunks with 20% overlap
  • A small open embedding model. Top 5 passages by similarity
  • An LLM writes a cited answer

No reranking. No query tricks. No framework.

The result

0.81

Correctness on 24 hand-labelled questions.

It also refused every question the text cannot answer.

A reasonable start, right? But it exposed many things.

What it exposed · 1

The Adi Parva is a complex form of storytelling.

One person, many names
Arjuna = Partha = Dhananjaya
One name, many people
Parikshit and Parikshita
Stories inside stories
A narrator retelling a lineage ten generations back
One life, scattered
Birth, family and deeds in different sections

Know your corpus before you index it. Chunks cannot see any of this, so the information has to be organised first: entity resolution, a knowledge graph, GraphRAG.

A good score can hide a weak stage.

I read the passages behind 12 graded answers:

Right section, wrong paragraph.
An irrelevant passage ranked #1.
The key sentence cut off at a chunk boundary: “And from Drupada wa…”
Why it matters: when retrieval misses, the model refuses questions it could answer. Karna's father: the passage naming him never reached it.
What it exposed · 4
Sameer SarnadBuilding AI Products

Calibrate the judge before you trust it.

1
An LLM judge scored the answers. I graded a sample blind and compared.
2
First round: 67% agreement on correctness.
3
Causes: name aliases and spelling variants. Fixed one change at a time.
4
Re-tested on fresh answers, then froze the judge.

A judge you have not checked is just another guess.
